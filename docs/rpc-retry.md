# Transient Telegram requests

Purple read requests use `PurpleRpcRetry` and the owned `PurpleRpcRequest` lifecycle. `PurpleRpcRequests` supplies the Android account/login adapter; discovery supplies an injected platform port to the same lifecycle. A request is retained for its retries and released at completion or cancellation. Read tokens are negative, separate from native request tokens used by dedicated writes.

`FLOOD_WAIT_X` and `FLOOD_PREMIUM_WAIT_X`, every 5xx response, internal-server errors, negative transport errors and the request's own timeout are transient. Permanent errors retain the feature's existing mapping. A transient never completes a read with final null, missing-message, unknown-facts or ineligible evidence.

A flood of at most 30 seconds permits one retry of the same request after X+1 seconds, provided that entire wait fits the remaining deadline. Only that wait extends the deadline. A subsequent flood, longer flood, or insufficient remaining deadline yields a typed transient outcome with `retryAt`. Server, transport and timeout outcomes use 5, 10, 20, 40 and then at most 60 seconds of backoff. The caller-owned stream schedules a fresh bounded network attempt at that time. Each logical request has one short retry allowance; each stream has at most one pending retry timer. Other reads in that stream queue behind it. Cancellation, account/login replacement, late replies and duplicate replies cannot dispatch a stale completion.

Discovery publishes Searching with `TelegramWait`, the retry time, the original read cache and posting held. A forced refresh queues without completing its caller with a wait status. RPC retry times use wall time; the service converts the remaining RPC wait into its monotonic clock for cooldown and watchdog comparisons, while UI formatting keeps wall time. A local-open or later local-phase watchdog preserves the same logical Request, final callback, before-post flag and borrowed account capability, and restarts full discovery. A retired borrowed capability is abandoned before enqueue or dispatch can retain it, and both watchdog paths retire a dead generation. Cancellation fences the old generation before touching its client, so a cancellation callback cannot affect a replacement request. A valid borrowed capability stays retained even when the cooldown has elapsed. Temporary Searching status is presentation only. It neither spends the deleted-mark search budget nor resolves a post or retirement gate with incomplete evidence.

Automatic record clients expose logical read ownership across queued, waiting and active attempts through both record and automatic wrappers. Each Pending has a `PurpleSyncReadScope`, whose ownership and completion clock cover only that Pending. It owns the history and exact-read tokens, including readMany, edit/retirement authorization and second metadata readback. Completion, cancellation and timeout retire each token once; cancellation during dispatch also cancels a token returned later. Unrelated reads cannot extend that Pending's local watchdog. Upload verification owns a separate Pending whose cancellation follows the upload operation's existing close path. An aggregate watchdog never replaces such a managed read with final null when its retry time has passed and the next network attempt is live. After the read retires, the separate uncovered local/download phase has a fresh five-minute bound. The dedicated FileLoader deadline is unchanged. The Sync chat screen, automatic-sync status, pinned-music scan, Markdown copy and last-seen privacy reads show wait time when surfaced. Privacy polling still ends at the original exposure deadline; read retries never extend that exposure.

## Dedicated writes

The three sync write handlers, file-part upload, sendMedia/editMessage and own-message deletion, keep their existing receipt, random-id, effect-ledger and uncertain-outcome handling. No write enters the read retry transport. Their transient classification is retained separately until the owner handles that outcome. This also covers a callback delivered after its retry time has elapsed.

For automatic sync, known transient uncertain outcomes leave the active checkpoint and staged bytes unchanged, release the operation, and schedule the existing SessionReady reconciliation event at `retryAt`. Reconciliation reads the remote evidence before any later effect. It does not consume core's finite unknown-outcome failure budget. Foreground events and local-save coalescing cannot bypass the cooldown. The cooldown is held in RAM for the captured login; after process restart the existing persistent checkpoint still forces reconciliation. No shared-core or ledger format changes are made.

The last-seen opening write already journals before dispatch and enters restoration on failure. It stays unchanged. The restoration write keeps that journal and the existing recovery scheduler. A transient defers recovery until at least `retryAt`, without consuming its permanent-failure counter or displaying a permanent restore failure. Its periodic recovery check can run later than the requested time. Permanent failures keep the existing behavior. No new transport retry, journal fields or privacy exposure time are introduced.

## Boundaries

The sync-chat creation executor remains qualification-held and has no connected production write transport. Its injected transport, resumable journal, persisted flood deadline and explicit retry policy are unchanged.

FileLoader-delegated downloads are outside this policy. They retain upstream retries and the existing Purple completion behavior. Their notification only carries an integer failure reason, so it cannot reliably identify a flood duration or a 5xx. A future observation-only hook would have to forward a sanitized error category and retry time at the upstream error boundary, without taking over FileLoader's retry policy or exposing a filename, peer, location or access hash.

The current core automatic-sync outcome has no transient retry-time value. A future shared-core improvement would accept a transient outcome plus retry time, preserve the pending effect, and reconcile without spending the permanent failure budget. The client scheduler above supplies that behavior without modifying core.

Host coverage is in `PurpleRpcRetryCases` and `PurpleSyncAutoCases`: deadline boundaries, both flood forms, bounded backoff, cancellation/login replacement, once-only completion, retained request ownership, real-TL Self full-info recovery, service cache preservation, split wall/monotonic clocks through a stalled disk continuation, retained owned/borrowed before-post completion, forced-caller queuing, expired borrowed-capability retirement before and after watchdog delivery, cancellation in both exact-read phases for each caller route, disable/re-enable, upload verification cleanup, scope isolation and late-token races, aggregate timeouts during wait and active attempts (including the shared boundary), the separate local/download bound, and ambiguous-write reconciliation with byte-identical checkpoints. No native qualification or Telegram request is part of these tests.
